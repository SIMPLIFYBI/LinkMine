    Method, and what is left out

    What the ratio is

    Reserves divided by one year of mine production. It is a measure of endowment against current extraction rate, not a forecast: reserves grow as exploration converts resources, and production rates change.

    Mine production only

    Refinery, smelter, plant and secondary output are excluded. China refines roughly 14 million tonnes of copper a year but mines 1.8 million; comparing refinery output to domestic reserves would have put China at 2.9 years instead of 22.8.

    Aggregates removed

    Publisher totals and residual buckets — “World total”, “Other countries” — are excluded, along with composite rows naming several countries at once.

    Vanadium is excluded

    The source publishes world vanadium reserves of 21,000 tonnes against 110,000 tonnes of annual world production — less than one year. The reserve figures appear to be in thousands while the unit column says tonnes. Until that is resolved, no vanadium ratio is shown.

    The rankings have a floor

    The mineral tables list only countries above a reporting threshold and sweep the rest into “Other countries”. A rank of 9th of 9 means ninth among countries large enough to be listed separately.

    Current-year figures are estimates

    The most recent year in each series is the publisher’s estimate for an incomplete year, and coal reserves are carried forward unchanged between editions rather than restated, so that series is a standing estimate.

    Commodities not ranked

    The ranking covers 29 commodities. Six are left out, for different reasons. Zirconium reserves are ZrO₂ content while production is gross weight, iron ore appears as both crude ore and iron content, and titanium splits into ilmenite and rutile, so none has a single like-for-like basis. Bauxite’s reserve and production lines are labelled differently in the source tables and have not yet been confirmed comparable. Tantalum is reported by only four countries for reserves, too few to rank. Vanadium has the unit problem described above. On every basis USGS publishes, Australia holds first or second place for reserves of all six, and first place for 2025 mine production of iron ore and zirconium. The headline claim applies to the 29 ranked commodities, not to everything USGS covers.

    Nickel is the outlier

    Australian nickel’s 556 years is the least stable figure here. It divides USGS’s 25 million tonne reserve figure by 45,000 tonnes of estimated 2025 mine output, less than half the 98,000 tonnes USGS gave for 2024. USGS also notes that only 11 million tonnes of Australia’s reserves are JORC-compliant or equivalent; on that figure the ratio is 244 years.
